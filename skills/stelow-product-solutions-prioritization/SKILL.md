# Solutions: Define and Prioritize

**Objective**: explore and prioritize possible product paths, balancing what is desirable for the audience with what is viable and strategic for the business.

---

## What to Evaluate

For each solution idea, draft:
1. An **innovation strategy** (differentiated, dominant, sustaining, discreet, or disruptive)
2. **Potential platforms** (the ecosystem or infrastructure behind the product)
3. **Business models**
4. **Main features** that address the core need
5. The **9 components for a desirable solution**
6. **Positive impact and risk level**

---

## 9 Components for a Desirable Solution

### 1. Optimize the Functional Outcome
Ensure the solution delivers the best possible functional result within the specific context of use. Understand the success criteria used to evaluate the job to be done.

### 2. Reduce Effort and Sacrifice (Daily Use and Lifecycle)
Even a functionally superior solution can be rejected if it's too difficult to use or requires too much sacrifice. This applies to every lifecycle activity: acquire, prepare for first use, maintain, dispose. Start by optimizing acquisition and first use.

### 3. Reduce Time to Achievement
Time is a valuable resource. Solutions that deliver results quickly or minimize the perception of time (through predictability and visibility of progress) are more attractive. This includes **TTFV — Time to First Value**.

### 4. Optimize the Investment-Value Relationship
Maximize perceived value relative to required investment. Two paths: reduce monetary price (making it more accessible) or increase perceived value (highlighting benefits, quality, exclusivity, return).

### 5. Increase the Perception That One Will Succeed
Build trust and reduce hesitation. Social proof, testimonials, and case studies show the solution works for others, decreasing perceived risk. Especially important when the solution is new, complex, or involves significant investment.

### 6. Maximize the Perception of Time Well Invested
Ensure the user feels the time spent with the solution was valuable and rewarding in itself. Goes beyond efficiency — focuses on meaningful engagement, learning, relaxation, clear progress, or intrinsic reward.

### 7. Offer Autonomy with Delimited Options
Offer autonomy to contribute (Ikea Effect) and customize the solution, but within a clear and simplified set of options to avoid decision fatigue (Paradox of Choice).

### 8. Create Positive, Meaningful, and Memorable Personal Experiences
Go beyond functionality to create emotional connections: surprise, delight, exclusivity, personalization, storytelling, aesthetics.

### 9. Increase the Sense of Belonging, Being Recognized and Valued
Address the human need for social connection and acceptance. Solutions that promote communities, facilitate recognition, or value individual contributions generate belonging, engagement, and advocacy.

### Complementary Elements
Evaluate if your solution also helps people to:
- **Enjoy the moment with satisfaction** (present pleasure, not just future result)
- **Increase social influence** over issues that impact their lives
- **Increase social status**
- **Increase purchasing power** for goods and services

## Positive Impact and Risk Matrix

### Positive Impact (Low → High)
- Market/niche/innovation impact
- Financial impact
- Operational impact
- Brand/reputation impact
- Social/environmental impact

### Risk Level (Low → High)
- **Probability of failure**: market risk, technological risk, execution risk, financial risk, regulatory/legal risk, operational risk
- **Impact of failure**: financial loss, time loss, reputation loss, opportunity loss, legal consequences

**Rule**: start with what has very high positive impact and very low risk.
