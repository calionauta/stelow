# Innovation Strategies

Every market has room for a new entrant. The key is choosing the right approach.

---

## The Five Strategies

### Differentiated Strategy

Serve over-served consumers. Charge more and deliver superior performance.

Introduce a high-performance solution to a saturated market, usually stealing market share from established competitors by being noticeably better on the dimensions that matter most to a specific segment.

- *Examples*: Dyson (more powerful vacuum), Apple (simpler, more elegant smartphone)
- **When to use**: you have a clear performance advantage; the market is crowded with mediocre options; a segment is willing to pay premium for excellence

### Dominant Strategy

Serve all consumers regardless of service degree. Charge less and deliver competitive performance.

Focus on price and operational efficiency to dominate by scale. The goal is not to be the best, but to be good enough at a price that competitors can't match.

- *Examples*: Dollar Shave Club, Nubank
- **When to use**: you can achieve cost advantages; the market is price-sensitive; operational efficiency is your strength

### Sustaining Strategy

Continue serving current customers with incremental improvements to existing products or services.

No major differentiations in performance or price — the goal is steady, predictable improvement. This is the default strategy for established market leaders.

- *Examples*: annual iPhone upgrades, yearly car model refreshes
- **When to use**: you have an established customer base; the market is stable; you need predictable revenue

### Discreet Strategy

Serve consumers with restricted options. Charge more for limited or worse performance.

Focus on niches or success criteria of ignored customers — people who have no better alternative and will accept a suboptimal solution because the market doesn't serve them.

- *Examples*: food sales and ATMs at airports (captive audience); lending to indebted people
- **When to use**: there's a captive audience; switching costs or constraints are high; you can charge for convenience or access

### Disruptive Strategy

Serve over-served consumers and non-consumers. Charge less with limited or worse performance initially.

Introduce changes that redefine how a job is accomplished. Start by serving the bottom of the market or non-consumers, then improve over time to move upmarket.

- *Examples*: Uber Pool (affordable rides for non-taxi users), early smartphones (less capable than PCs but more accessible)
- **When to use**: a segment is over-served by expensive/ complex solutions; non-consumers exist who would use a simpler, cheaper version

---

## Choosing a Strategy

| Your situation | Consider |
|---------------|----------|
| You have a clear performance edge | Differentiated |
| You can win on cost and scale | Dominant |
| You own an existing market | Sustaining |
| You found a captive or ignored niche | Discreet |
| You see non-consumers or over-served segments | Disruptive |

Each strategy positions you differently in the market. The best choice depends on your assets, the competitive landscape, and which customers are currently underserved.
