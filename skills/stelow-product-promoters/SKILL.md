# Promoters: Who Distributes Your Offer

Your promoters are the people and entities who tell others about your solution. The more leverage each promoter type gives you, the more scalable your growth.

---

## The 5 Types of Promoters

```
                     Leverage
                        ↑
    Employees ──── agencies ──── affiliates ──── customers ──── you
                        ↓
                  Effort to activate
```

**1. Yourself** — you do the outreach directly (cold, warm, content, ads). Zero leverage, maximum control. This is where every business starts.

**2. Customers (Referrals)** — customers who refer others. Highest quality leads: they buy at higher prices, stay longer, buy more often, and refer more. The most scalable free channel.

> "30% of your business from referrals at ANY scale still grows you 30%. It compounds into infinity." — Alex Hormozi

**3. Agencies** — companies that do prospecting as a service. You pay for the service, they bring leads. Good for scaling but expensive.

**4. Affiliates** — independent businesses/creators who promote to their audience in exchange for commission. They own their own advertising and bring engaged audiences. The most scalable paid channel.

> "An affiliate is a lead getter — an independent business that tells their audience to buy your stuff in exchange for money." — Alex Hormozi

**5. Employees** — hired prospectors/salespeople. Direct control but fixed cost.

## Hormozi's Lead Getters Framework (from $100M Leads)

**Core insight**: there are only two groups of people who can let strangers know about your stuff — YOU and OTHER PEOPLE. And there are more of them than there are of you.

### The 4 Levels of Leverage

| Scenario | What you do | Leverage |
|----------|------------|----------|
| 1. You are the lead getter | Core Four all day | 1x |
| 2. You get one lead getter | Train one person, they do it daily | 365x (one day of work = 365 days of output) |
| 3. You get many lead getters | Recruit one per day, they stack | Exponential |
| 4. You get a lead getter who gets lead getters | A recruiter finds promoters for you | Infinite |

> "It's not about going from 200 calls to 400 calls. It's about how can I make one call that gets me a thousand calls." — Alex Hormozi

## How to Get Customer Referrals (Hormozi's 7 Tactics)

Referrals are the highest-leverage promoter type because they cost nothing and produce customers who buy more, stay longer, and refer again.

### Tactic 1: Ask Immediately After the Sale

After closing, say: *"Who else do you know who might also benefit from this?"*

The key is specificity. Not "do you know anyone?" (yes/no trap) but "**who else** do you know **who would benefit** from this?" — this forces the customer to scan their network with a filter.

Hormozi's data: 1 in 3 customers will give 1-2 referrals when asked this way immediately after the sale. One salesperson using this single tactic outperformed an entire team by 30-50%.

### Tactic 2: Discount in Exchange for Introductions

Offer a discount on their next purchase in exchange for 3-5 warm introductions. The discount should be meaningful enough to motivate action but structured so you don't lose money.

### Tactic 3: Offer More Free Services

Give existing customers additional free service that they can gift to a friend. The friend experiences your value, and the customer feels like a hero for giving something valuable.

### Tactic 4: Offer Cash (Referral Fee)

Pay a straight referral fee. Simple, transparent, works. The amount should be meaningful — typically 20-50% of one month's value or a fixed amount per qualified referral.

### Tactic 5: Referral at the Success Moment

When a customer achieves a visible result, capture it. Take a photo, record a testimonial, then ask for a three-way introduction right there. The peak emotion of success is the highest-propensity moment for referrals.

> "You've literally demonstrated the result in front of someone they know, and then that person is making the introduction." — Alex Hormozi

### Tactic 6: Handwritten Card

Send a handwritten note to top customers asking for referrals. It's rare, personal, and memorable in a digital world. The effort signals genuine appreciation.

### Tactic 7: "Spouse Program" / Bring-a-Friend

Encourage customers to bring a spouse, partner, or friend. Structure it as "you'll be more successful together." One company ran an entire campaign simply saying "bring a friend — it's more likely you'll succeed" in every message.

Result: 25-30% of all campaign customers came from word-of-mouth.

### The Math of Referrals

If you have a recurring business with 10% monthly churn:
- 10% referral rate → break even (stable)
- 20% referral rate → 2:1 growth (doubling)
- The ratio of referral % to churn % is your stable-at-rest growth rate

## How to Contract Affiliates

### The Affiliate Offer

Structure the offer to benefit three parties simultaneously:

| Party | What they get |
|-------|--------------|
| Affiliate | Commission (% of sale, flat fee, or both) |
| Their audience | A compelling offer they'll thank the affiliate for |
| You | New customers at a predictable CPA |

> "If you really want affiliates to work, you have to make it so good that it hurts." — Alex Hormozi

### The Affiliate Machine (7 Components)

1. **Affiliate sales page** — sell them on why they should promote you. Explain prizes, requirements, and how you support them.
2. **Tiered rewards** — different prizes for different performance levels (top 10, top 50, 10+ referrals, just registered).
3. **Pre-made collateral** — emails, posts, ads, and scripts they can copy-paste. Lower friction = more promoters.
4. **Custom attribution link** — make tracking visible and trustworthy.
5. **Leaderboard** — real-time ranking creates competition and proves attribution.
6. **Onboarding + fast reward** — immediate custom link, quick first win to build momentum.
7. **Feedback loop** — share winning hooks and angles with all affiliates to lift everyone's performance.

### Example Affiliate Incentive Structure (from Hormozi's $100M Money Models Launch)

| Tier | Requirement | Prize |
|------|-------------|-------|
| 🥇 Top 10 | Highest referrals | Fireside Chat + Podcast at Vegas HQ, Private Dinner with founders |
| 🥈 Top 50 | High performance | Full-day Vegas event, Live Podcast Session, Live Q&A |
| 🎟️ 10+ referrals | Minimum activation | Unreleased audiobook, raffle for event seats |
| 🎁 Just registered | Sign up | Behind-the-scenes assets, swipe files, playbook |

## How to Contract Agencies

Agencies are professional lead getters. They bring existing processes and infrastructure.

**What to look for:**
- Proven experience in your niche
- Transparent reporting and attribution
- Performance-based components in the contract

**How to approach:**
- Start with a small test campaign (1 month, limited budget)
- Define clear KPIs and lead qualification criteria
- Negotiate a mix of retainer + performance bonus

---

## How to Contract Employees for Prospecting

Employees are the lowest-leverage promoter (fixed cost, management overhead) but offer the most control.

**When to hire:**
- You've validated that the channel works (you did it yourself first)
- Volume exceeds your capacity
- You can train and document the process

**The key**: employees should execute a system you've already proven. They amplify — they don't discover.

---

## How to Be Your Own First Promoter (Core Four)

Before contracting others, you must prove the model yourself:

| Channel | Effort | Leverage | Best for |
|---------|--------|----------|----------|
| Cold Outreach | High | Low | B2B, high-ticket |
| Warm Outreach | Medium | Medium | Existing network |
| Free Content | Medium | High (compounds) | Authority building |
| Paid Ads | Low (per execution) | Medium | Proven offers |

**Rule**: don't scale a promoter type until you've validated it yourself first.

## Quick Reference: Which Promoter to Use When

| Your situation | Start with | Then scale to |
|---------------|------------|---------------|
| Pre-revenue, no audience | Yourself (Core Four) | Customer referrals |
| Have paying customers | Customer referrals + Affiliates | Agencies |
| Proven offer, need volume | Paid Ads + Affiliates | Employees |
| High ticket, relationship sale | Yourself (warm outreach) | Agencies + Referrals |
| Low ticket, high volume | Affiliates + Content | Paid Ads |

## Appendix: Stimulating Marketplace Supply and Demand

When your business is a marketplace (connecting two sides), these 19 tactics help you reach critical mass.

### 19 Tactics

1. **Get the harder side first.** When the harder side (supply or demand) reaches its critical activity point, network effects kick in and value is created organically for the easier side.

2. **Strongly attract to a niche and repeat.** Find the small groups in your community that care most about your market ("white-hot center"). Usually discovered by going broad enough to collect data that shows the most activity.

3. **Subsidize the most valuable side of the market.** Pay in cash to the most valuable side — or to the most valuable niche within the most valuable side — to join the marketplace.

4. **Make supply look bigger with automation.** Start the supply side by aggregating as much web data as possible to create a "perceived aura of activity".

5. **Build one side as an email list.** An easy and cheap way to start a market, especially if many buyers are also sellers and vice versa.

6. **Hold meetups and gatherings.** Hard to scale. In the beginning, however, they can be effective for generating community, demonstrating activity, and getting direct customer feedback.

7. **Create a SaaS tool for one side of the market.** When you give or sell a SaaS tool to one side of the market, it helps keep them in the market, giving time to attract the other side.

8. **Provide software to third parties who can bring one side of the market.**

9. **Find a giant user for initial supply or demand.**

10. **Make only one side change their behavior.**

11. **Make something free suddenly.** Transforming something that used to cost money into something free will attract users.

12. **Create a product first and then open a market.**

13. **Connect the two sides manually.**

14. **Prefer markets where buyers are also sellers.** Avoid building a single-sided market altogether.

15. **Create exclusive access.** Restrict access and create fear of missing supply or demand can generate strong word of mouth. However, this almost never works.

16. **Define a geographic constraint.** Easier to generate a lot of activity when limiting the operating area at launch.

17. **Define a time constraint.** One can program enthusiasm into the market with time constraints.

18. **Define a demand constraint.**

19. **Pay users with tokens.** If you create a token for the market, you can pay people to join, even when there's no activity.
